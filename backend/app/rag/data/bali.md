# Bali Destination Guide

## Overview
Bali is an Indonesian island known for beaches, rice terraces, temples,
and a strong wellness/yoga tourism scene, particularly around Ubud.

## Best Time to Visit
The dry season (April to October) is generally best for beach and
outdoor activities. The wet season (November to March) still has plenty
of sunshine but with short, heavy afternoon showers.

## Transport
Bali does not have a metro system. Most travelers rely on private
drivers, scooters, or ride-hailing apps (Gojek, Grab) which are cheap and
widely used, though traffic in the south (Seminyak, Kuta, Canggu) can be
heavy. Ubud and the south coast are roughly 1-1.5 hours apart by car.

## Culture & Etiquette
Bali is predominantly Hindu, distinct from the rest of Indonesia.
Modest dress (covering shoulders and knees) is expected when visiting
temples, and a sarong is often provided or required at temple entrances.
Small offerings (canang sari) are placed on the ground outside homes and
shops daily and should not be stepped on.

## Money
The local currency is the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR), with large
denominations that can be confusing for first-time visitors. Cash is
still preferred for warungs (small local restaurants) and markets; cards
are accepted at hotels and larger restaurants.

## Neighborhoods & Highlights
- Ubud: rice terraces, art markets, yoga retreats, Monkey Forest.
- Seminyak/Canggu: beach clubs, surf, cafes, nightlife.
- Uluwatu: cliffside temples, surf breaks, sunset views.
- Nusa Penida (day trip by boat): dramatic cliffs and beaches.

## Airport
Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) is in the south near Kuta,
roughly 45-90 minutes from Ubud depending on traffic.
